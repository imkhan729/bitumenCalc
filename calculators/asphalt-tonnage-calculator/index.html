What is Asphalt Tonnage?

Tonnage is the total weight of asphalt mix required for your project. It equals compacted volume (area × thickness) multiplied by the mix bulk density.

Standard Mix Densities

Dense HMA: 2,300–2,400 kg/m³ (144–150 lb/ft³). SMA: 2,350–2,450 kg/m³. Porous/OGFC: 1,950–2,150 kg/m³. Use approved JMF values for tenders.

Wastage & Safety Factor

Always order 5–10% extra for compaction variations, cold joints, and paver transitions. Large highway jobs use 5%; small or curved driveways need 8–10%.

Engineering Methodology

The Asphalt Tonnage Formula Explained

Understanding the calculation helps you verify tender estimates and prevent costly plant re-delivery charges.

Standard Pavement Takeoff Formula
Weight (t) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (m) × Compacted Density (kg/m³) ÷ 1,000
1

Calculate Volume

Multiply the paved area (length × width in metres) by the compacted layer thickness converted to metres. For example: a 200 m road, 7 m wide, at 75 mm (0.075 m) gives 200 × 7 × 0.075 = 105 m³.

2

Apply Bulk Density

Multiply volume by compacted density. Dense HMA is typically 2,350 kg/m³: 105 m³ × 2,350 kg/m³ = 246,750 kg. Divide by 1,000 to convert to 246.75 metric tonnes.

3

Add Site Allowance

Theoretical takeoff never accounts for transverse joints, paver hoppers, or minor grade variations. Add 5–10% extra: for 246.75 t, adding 7% means ordering 264 tonnes.

Material Partition: Bitumen Binder vs Mineral Aggregate

Total mix weight divides into pure bitumen binder and graded mineral aggregate. At 5.5% bitumen content: 246.75 t × 0.055 = 13.57 t of bitumen and 246.75 − 13.57 = 233.18 t of aggregate.

🔥 Bitumen Binder: 13.57 t (5.5%) 🪨 Mineral Aggregate & Filler: 233.18 t (94.5%)

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between tonnes and short tons in asphalt?

A metric tonne equals 1,000 kg (2,204.6 lbs). A US short ton equals 2,000 lbs (907 kg). Asphalt suppliers in the US typically quote in short tons, while most of the rest of the world (including Australia, UK, and Canada) use metric tonnes. The difference is about 10%: 1 metric tonne = 1.102 short tons. Always confirm which unit your supplier is quoting in to avoid costly ordering errors.

What density should I use to calculate asphalt tonnage?

Standard Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA/dense-graded) has a compacted density of approximately 2,300–2,400 kg/m³, commonly estimated at 2,350 kg/m³. Stone Matrix Asphalt (SMA) is slightly heavier at 2,300–2,380 kg/m³. Open-graded mixes are lighter at 1,950–2,100 kg/m³. For the most accurate results, use the actual density from your project's mix design or the plant's mix design report.

How many tonnes of asphalt per square metre?

For a 50 mm (2-inch) thick compacted HMA layer at 2,350 kg/m³: 0.05 m × 2,350 kg/m³ = 117.5 kg/m² = 0.1175 tonnes/m². A 100 m² area at 50 mm thickness requires approximately 11.75 tonnes. For a 25 mm (1-inch) layer, halve that to 5.875 tonnes. Our calculator handles this automatically — just enter your dimensions and density.

Should I add a waste factor to my asphalt tonnage calculation?

Yes. Always add a waste/overage factor when ordering asphalt. For straightforward machine-laid rectangular areas, add 5–8%. For irregular shapes, hand-work areas, driveways with curves, or projects with multiple thin lifts, use 10–15%. Material left in trucks (truck tare), cold joints, and compaction density variation also contribute to waste. It's better to have a small surplus than to run short and wait for a second delivery.

How many tonnes of asphalt do I need for a driveway?

For a typical residential driveway, a 50 mm compacted HMA layer at 2,350 kg/m³ requires approximately 0.1175 tonnes per m². A single-car driveway of 3 m × 10 m (30 m²) needs around 3.5 tonnes. A double driveway of 6 m × 12 m (72 m²) needs approximately 8.5 tonnes. Add 10% for edges and waste, giving a final order of around 9.5 tonnes. Use our Square Feet Calculator if you measured in imperial units.

How do I convert square metres to tonnes of asphalt?

Multiply the area in m² by the thickness in metres, then by the density in kg/m³, then divide by 1,000 to get tonnes. For example: 500 m² × 0.05 m (50 mm) × 2,350 kg/m³ ÷ 1,000 = 58.75 tonnes. If you only know the area in square metres and need a quick estimate, a rule of thumb for 50 mm HMA is approximately 0.12 t/m². Our Square Metres Calculator automates this conversion.

What bitumen content percentage should I use?

Standard Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) contains approximately 4.5–6.5% bitumen binder by total mix weight. Surface course mixes tend towards the higher end (5.5–6.5%) for durability and waterproofing, while base course mixes may be lower (4–5%). Stone Mastic Asphalt (SMA) uses 6–7.5% due to its gap-graded aggregate skeleton requiring more binder to fill voids. Always use the bitumen content from your approved mix design rather than a default estimate for procurement calculations.
