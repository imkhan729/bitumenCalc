Formula

Square Feet to Tons Formula

The direct sq ft to tons conversion uses three inputs: area, thickness, and density. No intermediate steps needed — the formula returns US short tons directly.

Weight (short tons) = Area (sq ft) × Thickness (in) ÷ 12 × Density (lb/ft³) ÷ 2,000
1

Measure Area in Square Feet

Multiply length × width in feet. Example: A 2-car residential driveway 50 ft × 20 ft = 1,000 sq ft. For irregular shapes, break into rectangles and sum the areas. Use a measuring tape or count paces (1 pace ≈ 2.5 ft) for a quick field estimate.

2

Apply Thickness & Density

Divide thickness by 12 to convert inches to feet. Multiply by standard density (145 lb/ft³ for most HMA). 1,000 × (2 ÷ 12) × 145 = 24,167 lb. Divide by 2,000 to get short tons: 12.1 short tons. An illustrative 5% allowance gives 12.69 US short tons; choose an allowance for the actual job.

3

Quick Rule of Thumb

At 145 lb/ft³, a simplified rule: 0.0060417 US short tons per sq ft per inch of thickness. For a 2-inch layer: multiply sq ft × 0.0121. For a 3-inch layer: sq ft × 0.0182. These shortcuts work for quick mental estimates. For precise binder/aggregate breakdown, use the full Asphalt Tonnage Calculator.

💡
Density Matters

The 145 lb/ft³ default applies to most dense-graded HMA. Open-graded mixes (OGFC, porous asphalt) run 115–125 lb/ft³. SMA runs 145–155 lb/ft³. Cold mix patching compounds run around 130–135 lb/ft³. Using the wrong density can cause a 10–15% ordering error, so always confirm with your plant's certified mix design. For metric projects, use the Square Metres Calculator.

Guide

How to Use This Calculator

1

Enter Square Footage of Your Project

Type the total area in square feet. For rectangular areas, multiply length × width. For driveways with irregular shapes, break the area into rectangles and add the totals. Measure to the nearest foot for practical accuracy — sub-foot precision rarely changes the tonnage result meaningfully.

2

Specify Compacted Thickness in Inches

Enter the finished compacted depth in inches. Residential driveways typically require 2–3 inches of asphalt; commercial driveways and parking lots need 3–4 inches; roads and heavy-duty areas may need 4–8 inches total in multiple lifts. Use the compacted thickness, not the loose mat thickness.

3

Get Direct Tons Output

The calculator returns US short tons directly from your square footage input. The formula is: tons = (sq ft × thickness in inches × density lb/ft³) ÷ (12 × 2000). Round up to the nearest half-ton for your order and add a 5–10% overage for waste and compaction variation.

The cubic-yard conversion trips people up more often than the square-foot one. Tons per cubic yard works through it with the densities spelled out.

References

Where the conversion factors come from

Unit conversions are exact by definition; the densities they get multiplied by are not. Those come from the references below, and from your mix design sheet if you have one.

  • Asphalt Institute, MS-2 Asphalt Mix Design Methods, 7th edition
  • ASTM D2041/D2041M-19 — Theoretical Maximum Specific Gravity and Density of Asphalt Mixtures (the Rice test)
  • ASTM D2726/D2726M-21 — Bulk Specific Gravity and Density of Non-Absorptive Compacted Asphalt Mixtures

How many square feet does a ton of asphalt cover?

At 145 lb/ft³, a US short ton covers 82.8 ft² at 2 inches. A metric tonne covers about 91.2 ft² at the same depth and density. The unit, compacted depth and density must accompany every coverage figure.

Area (ft²) = US short tons × 2,000 ÷ [density (lb/ft³) × depth (in) ÷ 12]

For 1,000 ft² at 2 inches, the result is 12.083 short tons or 10.962 metric tonnes before allowance. Read the main calculator's t value as metric tonnes; its secondary weight states US short tons. For density selection see the asphalt density guide; for volume conversions see tons per cubic yard.
